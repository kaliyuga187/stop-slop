# What I Learned After Burning Out Twice in Three Years

Quick story: in 2023 I quit a job I loved because I couldn't get out of bed anymore. In 2025 I did it again at a different company. Both times I told myself it was situational. Both times I was wrong.

Sound familiar? If you're reading this, it probably does. Burnout has become so normalized in tech that people wear it as a badge of seriousness. The reality is darker. Burnout is a slow-motion injury, and the second time hits harder than the first.

Here's what I've learned. Take it or leave it.

Unpopular opinion: most burnout isn't caused by working too many hours. It's caused by working on the wrong things. I was billing 50-hour weeks at jobs where I genuinely loved the work and never felt tired. I've billed 35-hour weeks at jobs where I was vaporized by Thursday. The variable wasn't volume. It was alignment.

When your daily tasks aren't connected to anything you care about, every hour costs more than it should. Your brain is constantly translating between what you're doing and why you're doing it. That translation tax is the real killer. People assume hours are the unit. Hours aren't the unit. Meaning is the unit.

The second thing I've learned is that recovery is non-linear. After my first burnout, I took two months off and felt mostly better. After the second, two months didn't touch it. It's been seven months and I'm just now starting to feel like myself. Recovery time scales with damage, and damage compounds.

The third thing is the hardest. The thing that made me burn out the second time was the belief that I'd already learned my lesson. I was so confident I knew the warning signs that I dismissed them when they showed up dressed differently. The fatigue was real but I labeled it "just a busy quarter." The dread on Sunday nights was real but I labeled it "the project is in a hard phase." Sound familiar?

The takeaway: don't trust your own pattern-matching after burnout. Trust the people who watched you go through it. They see the warning signs sooner than you do. They will tell you. Listen.

It's 2026 and the industry is finally talking about this stuff. But talking isn't fixing. The companies that fix it are the ones where leaders model rest, not the ones with a meditation app subscription. Watch what they do, not what they say.

If you're in the early stages: please slow down. The work will be there. You might not be.
