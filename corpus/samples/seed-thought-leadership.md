# Why Most Remote Teams Are Still Failing in 2026

Quick story: I joined a company last year that was fully remote, fully async, and fully convinced they had figured it out. Six months in, the engineering team was burned out, product was missing deadlines, and the founder was quietly hiring an office manager. Sound familiar?

It's 2026 and most remote teams still operate like it's 2019 with extra Zoom calls. Leaders genuinely believe that adding more meetings will fix communication problems. The reality is more complicated.

Hot take: remote work isn't broken. The management practices we ported over from offices are broken. Companies are essentially running in-office playbooks with worse latency and calling it transformation.

Here's the thing: when you remove the office, you remove all the implicit coordination that happened in hallways. Status updates, quick clarifications, vibe checks. Inevitably, leaders try to replace those with formal processes. More standups. More syncs. More dashboards. The team drowns in coordination overhead and ships less.

The teams that actually work remotely have done something fundamentally different. They've replaced synchronous coordination with written artifacts and trust. Not with more meetings. Not with surveillance software. With documents that anyone can read at any time.

Think about it: the highest-leverage thing a manager can do in a remote company is write clearly. Yet most managers were promoted because they were good at the office version of management, which is mostly about presence and verbal facilitation. Those skills don't transfer.

What's the path forward? Three shifts:

1. Hire writers, not talkers. Look at someone's writing samples before their interview loop.
2. Kill recurring meetings every quarter. Make people justify their existence.
3. Measure shipping cadence, not activity. Activity metrics literally always reward the wrong behavior.

Sound familiar? It should. We've been saying this since 2020. Companies that have done it are thriving. Companies that haven't are quietly rebuilding offices and pretending it was the plan all along.

TL;DR: Remote work isn't the problem. Management practices that don't translate to async are. Fix the writing culture and the rest follows.
