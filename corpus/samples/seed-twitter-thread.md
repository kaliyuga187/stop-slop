# 7 Writing Habits That Made Me a Better Engineer (a thread)

1/ I've shipped at three startups and one FAANG and the single biggest career unlock wasn't a framework or a language. It was learning to write. Here's what changed for me.

2/ Quick aside: when I say "writing," I don't mean blog posts. I mean docs, design proposals, postmortems, Slack messages. The boring stuff. Sound familiar? It's the stuff most engineers skip.

3/ Habit one: write before you code. I now spend roughly 30% of any non-trivial project on a written design doc before touching the editor. The doc forces me to actually think. Code lets me pretend I'm thinking.

4/ Habit two: write for the reader who has no context. I used to write docs assuming the reader knew what I knew. Now I assume they're smart but uninformed. Adds 20% length, saves hours of back-and-forth.

5/ Hot take: most "communication problems" on engineering teams are writing problems. The team isn't bad at communicating. They're bad at writing. These aren't the same thing and we keep conflating them.

6/ Habit three: edit in passes. First pass for content. Second pass for structure. Third pass for sentence-level clarity. If I try to do all three at once I end up with a mediocre draft that I'm too attached to fix.

7/ Habit four: kill adverbs. "Quickly," "really," "essentially," "basically." Every one of them is a tell that you don't trust your verb. Strengthen the verb instead.

8/ Habit five: prefer short sentences over long ones. Long sentences hide imprecise thinking. Short sentences expose it. You can't bullshit yourself in a five-word sentence. Sound familiar? Hemingway figured this out a century ago.

9/ Habit six: every document needs a TL;DR at the top. If you can't write a two-sentence summary of your own doc, you don't understand what you're proposing yet. Go back and figure it out before publishing.

10/ Unpopular opinion: AI writing tools are making engineers worse at writing, not better. The struggle of finding the right word is where the thinking happens. Outsource the struggle, lose the thinking.

11/ Habit seven: read your writing aloud before sending. If it sounds weird in your head, it'll sound weird to the reader. This single trick has caught more bugs in my prose than any spellchecker.

12/ TL;DR: better writing is the highest-leverage skill in engineering. It compounds. Start today. Future you will thank present you.
